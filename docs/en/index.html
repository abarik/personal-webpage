I have been an assistant professor at the Department of Social and Work Psychology, Institute of Psychology, Faculty of Humanities, University of Debrecen since 2005. I trained as a software engineer and mathematician, and received my PhD from the University of Debrecen in 2013. My research covers statistical data analysis, speech research and education, in particular gamification and algorithmic thinking.

Research areas

  • Statistics and R

    Data management and statistical analysis in R; the statistical background of evaluating development programmes.

  • Speech research

    Acoustic properties of Hungarian speech: formants, coarticulation, word duration in spontaneous speech.

  • Gamification and education

    Gamification in education; a digital course supplement for language learning of disadvantaged pupils (HANNA); learner motivation in low-SES regions.

  • Algorithmic skills

    Measuring and developing the algorithmic skills of school and university students; teaching spreadsheet management (Sprego).

More about my research

Teaching

  • Statistics

    The language of psychological research: how to describe data, and when we can draw reliable conclusions from it.

  • Computing

    The digital foundations of academic work: spreadsheets, reference management and the use of library resources.

  • Artificial intelligence

    How do today's AI systems work, and what do they mean for psychology?

  • Social networks

    The structure of relationships says a lot: who is connected to whom, who sits at the centre of a network, and how information spreads through it.

  • Knowledge space theory

    Knowledge space theory models how pieces of knowledge build on each other within a subject, and how this lets us determine precisely what someone knows.
